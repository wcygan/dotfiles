## Bartosz Ciechanowski

Links:
- Gears: https://ciechanow.ski/gears/
- Gears article script: https://ciechanow.ski/js/gears.js
- Shared helpers: https://ciechanow.ski/js/base.js
- Blog archive: https://ciechanow.ski/archives/

Patterns to study:
- Empty article containers become live demos after page load.
- Sliders map directly to physical or geometric parameters.
- Animations can be paused globally and locally.
- Concepts unfold from idealized simple shapes to realistic mechanisms.
- Labels, force vectors, traces, and ghost states appear only when they support the current explanation.

Use for: mechanical systems, geometry, optics, camera models, 3D diagrams, layered derivations.

## Bret Victor

Links:
- Explorable Explanations: https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/
- Learnable Programming: https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/

Patterns to study:
- The reader manipulates the representation while reading.
- Controls are part of the sentence-level explanation, not a separate app panel.
- Immediate feedback is more valuable than feature depth.

Use for: deciding what parameter the reader should touch and how prose should react.

## Nicky Case

Links:
- LOOPY: https://ncase.me/loopy/
- Home/projects: https://ncase.me/

Patterns to study:
- Friendly controls over a compact systems model.
- The state is editable, not just animated.
- Visual metaphors stay consistent across examples.

Use for: systems thinking, causal loops, approachable controls, playful but precise interaction.

## Amit Patel / Red Blob Games

Links:
- Red Blob Games: https://www.redblobgames.com/
- Interactive graph search: https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html
- Hexagonal grids: https://www.redblobgames.com/grids/hexagons/

Patterns to study:
- Small diagrams are embedded directly beside the relevant explanation.
- Algorithms expose intermediate state, not just final output.
- Drag handles and hover state make abstract data structures tactile.

Use for: spatial algorithms, grid geometry, pathfinding, map generation, interaction density.

## Matthew Conlen

Links:
- Website: https://matthewconlen.com/
- Explorable essays with collaborators: https://parametric.press/

Patterns to study:
- Narrative and interactive figures share one reading path.
- Math and visualization stay close enough that readers can connect them.

Use for: research-like explanations and articles where the model is the argument.

## Steven Wittens / Acko.net / MathBox

Links:
- Acko.net: https://acko.net/
- Making MathBox: https://acko.net/blog/making-mathbox/
- MathBox demos: https://mathbox.org/

Patterns to study:
- WebGL and Three.js can be wrapped in higher-level math primitives.
- Viewports and coordinate transforms are first-class concepts.
- Labels and equations need special handling in 3D graphics.

Use for: 3D math, coordinate systems, shader-backed visuals, presentation-quality spatial diagrams.

## Jason Davies

Links:
- Visualizations: https://www.jasondavies.com/

Patterns to study:
- Geometry-heavy interactives use precise construction and direct manipulation.
- Projection and transform demos benefit from many small, focused examples.

Use for: computational geometry, projections, curves, maps, and algorithmic visualizations.

## Grant Sanderson / 3Blue1Brown

Links:
- 3Blue1Brown: https://www.3blue1brown.com/
- Manim Community: https://www.manim.community/

Patterns to study:
- Build visual vocabulary before layering abstraction.
- Camera motion and object transforms should follow the idea, not decorate it.
- Reuse colors and spatial metaphors across a whole explanation.

Use for: math exposition and sequencing a concept from concrete to abstract.

## Falstad Simulators

Links:
- Math and physics applets: https://www.falstad.com/mathphysics.html
- Circuit simulator: https://www.falstad.com/circuit/

Patterns to study:
- Dense controls can work when the audience expects an instrument.
- Simulators expose many presets and let users change parameters freely.
- Visual feedback is continuous and tied to the actual simulation model.

Use for: circuits, oscillations, fields, waves, filters, and other parameter-rich simulations.
